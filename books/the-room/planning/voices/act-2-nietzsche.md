- Questions in volleys, answered by a dash or left hanging.

**Signature moves**

1. Ask the value of the value. Exemplar: wir haben eine **Kritik** der moralischen Werthe nöthig, **der Werth dieser Werthe ist selbst erst einmal in Frage zu stellen** - "we need a *critique* of moral values; *the value of these values must itself, for once, be called into question*" (GM-Vorrede-6). Every committee value ("good paper," "mature," "the right direction") gets this question; the climax turns it on standardization itself as the act's question, whether standardizing this is worth the cost (A2-20).
2. Let etymology testify. Exemplar: das deutsche Wort „schlecht“ selber: als welches mit „schlicht“ identisch ist - "the German word *schlecht* (bad) itself, which is identical with *schlicht* (plain)" (GM-I-4). The room's own words ("orphan" paper, "clean" history, "mature") are made to confess where they came from. (2026-10-16: *revidere*, Second Essay section 2; see A2-10.)
3. The right to name. Exemplar: Das Herrenrecht, Namen zu geben, geht so weit, dass man sich erlauben sollte, den Ursprung der Sprache selbst als Machtäusserung der Herrschenden zu fassen: sie sagen „das **ist** das und das“, sie siegeln jegliches Ding und Geschehen mit einem Laute ab und nehmen es dadurch gleichsam in Besitz. - "The lordly right to give names goes so far that one should allow oneself to grasp the origin of language itself as an expression of power by those who rule: they say 'this *is* such and such,' they seal every thing and every event with a sound and thereby, as it were, take possession of it" (GM-I-2). The act spends it once, on the Newcomer (Second Essay section 3): the room folds her idea into an established paper and gives her a line in its acknowledgments, a name that takes possession. Paraphrase it in his voice, without quotation marks.
4. Ironic courtesy before the turn. Exemplar: Diese englischen Psychologen, denen man bisher auch die einzigen Versuche zu danken hat, es zu einer Entstehungsgeschichte der Moral zu bringen, - sie geben uns mit sich selbst kein kleines Räthsel auf - "These English psychologists, to whom we also owe the only attempts so far to arrive at a history of the origin of morality - they set us no small riddle in themselves" (GM-I-1). The act's opening pastiche ("Those committee psychologists...") is this move; keep it (edits A2-1). Round three restores its payoff, which turns the original inside out: "My English psychologists, at least, pushed the partie honteuse of our inner world to the front; these push forward the most becoming face of theirs." It paraphrases GM-I-1 without quotation marks, and without it readers who don't know the original miss the joke.
5. The interrupted address. Exemplar: Versteht man mich?… Hat man mich verstanden?… **„Schlechterdings nicht! mein Herr!“** - Fangen wir also von vorne an. - "Am I understood?... Have I been understood?... 'Not in the least, my dear sir!' - Then let us begin again from the beginning" (GM-III-1). At most once per essay, at a turn. The First Essay spends it on the dark workshop's listener (A2-25), and the Third Essay on "Not in the least, sir?" (Third Essay section 3).
6. The animal parable. Exemplar: Dass die Lämmer den grossen Raubvögeln gram sind, das befremdet nicht: nur liegt darin kein Grund, es den grossen Raubvögeln zu verargen, dass sie sich kleine Lämmer holen. - "That lambs bear a grudge against great birds of prey is not strange: only it is no reason to blame the great birds of prey for taking little lambs" (GM-I-13). Animals bear the structure (water animals, birds of prey, the animal entitled to promise); no user and no person is ever the prey.
7. Diagnosis by physiology. Exemplar: Er bringt Salben und Balsam mit, es ist kein Zweifel; aber erst hat er nöthig, zu verwunden, um Arzt zu sein - "He brings salves and balm, no doubt; but first he needs to wound, in order to be a physician" (GM-III-15). Health and sickness describe the institution's habits, never a person's mind or body (G11).
8. Compression at the climax. Exemplar: Wir sind uns unbekannt, wir Erkennenden, wir selbst uns selbst - "We are unknown to ourselves, we knowers, we ourselves to ourselves" (GM-Vorrede-1). One turned, chiastic sentence closes a tempo feroce; the act's last turn is the void sentence, "man would sooner have the void for his purpose than be void of purpose" (Golffing's rendering of GM-III-28; see the quote ledger, Q25, Q29, and Q34). It is the act's refrain: the Third Essay sets it as its epigraph, quotes it again in section 3, varies it in section 5 as the human will's horror vacui ("it would sooner have the void for its purpose than be void of purpose"), and closes on it with the "would sooner" chain (restored 2026-10-16 at the author's request).

**Narrative facts that bear on voice**

- The Genealogy's preface is signed **Sils-Maria**, Oberengadin, im Juli 1887 (GM-Vorrede-8). The opening line, "I write this in the clear air of the Engadine," is literally where and when he wrote it, and 1887 less 1513 gives the opening's three hundred and seventy-four years. The title page calls the book *Eine Streitschrift*, a polemic.
- In *Ecce Homo* he described each essay's tempo: Jedes Mal ein Anfang, der irre führen soll, kühl, wissenschaftlich, ironisch selbst, absichtlich Vordergrund, absichtlich hinhaltend. Allmählich mehr Unruhe; vereinzeltes Wetterleuchten; sehr unangenehme Wahrheiten aus der Ferne her mit dumpfem Gebrumm laut werdend, - bis endlich ein tempo feroce erreicht ist, wo Alles mit ungeheurer Spannung vorwärts treibt. - "Each time a beginning meant to mislead: cool, scientific, even ironic, deliberately foreground, deliberately holding back. Gradually more unrest; scattered sheet lightning; very unpleasant truths growing audible from far off with a dull rumble - until at last a tempo feroce is reached in which everything drives forward with tremendous tension" (*Ecce Homo*, "Why I Write Such Good Books," on the Genealogy). The act's sections should move the same way.
- The same *Ecce Homo* passage says the ascetic ideal holds power faute de mieux, - weil es das einzige Ideal bisher war, weil es keinen Concurrenten hatte - "because it was the only ideal so far, because it had no competitor." That is the honest source for Third Essay section 4's "no alternative" (A2-18).
- His preface says of the Third Essay: dieser Abhandlung ist ein Aphorismus vorangestellt, sie selbst ist dessen Commentar - "an aphorism is set before this essay, and the essay itself is its commentary" (GM-Vorrede-8). The act's Third Essay may treat its epigraph the same way. The same section asks the reader for **das Wiederkäuen**, rumination: the voice may slow down and chew on one sentence.
- Kinship with the Florentine: Thukydides und, vielleicht, der principe Machiavell's sind mir selber am meisten verwandt durch den unbedingten Willen, sich Nichts vorzumachen und die Vernunft in der Realität zu sehn - "Thucydides and, perhaps, Machiavelli's Prince are most closely related to me by the unconditional will to deceive oneself about nothing and to see reason in reality" (*Twilight of the Idols*, "What I Owe to the Ancients" 2). In *Beyond Good and Evil* 28 he praised the Florentine's allegrissimo and wrote that his Prince "die trockne feine Luft von Florenz athmen lässt," lets one breathe the dry, fine air of Florence, a natural partner for the Engadine air of the opening. Scholars dispute whether he read the Prince closely (Sommer's commentary on *Beyond Good and Evil* 28), so claim kinship and praise, not study. The act's prose drops the "perhaps" ("the Florentine, my kinsman in the unconditional will to deceive oneself about nothing"; audit C1, under the historical license for the narrators' own sayings); the source quotation above keeps it.
- One German word: he uses *Umwerthung* both for the priestly inversion ("eine radikale Umwerthung von deren Werthen," GM-I-7) and for his own planned work ("Versuch einer Umwerthung aller Werthe," GM-III-27). The part's "transvaluation" (the public's) versus "revaluation" (the Author's) exists only in English. What the public performs he calls, in First Essay 10, "Umkehrung des werthesetzenden Blicks," the reversal of the value-positing glance.
- The animal of Second Essay 1 is one that "versprechen darf," that is entitled to promise. Samuel's "can promise" drops the entitlement, which is exactly what the room confers.
- He died on 25 August 1900 at the Villa Silberblick in Weimar (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, "Nietzsche's Life and Works"); he had spent many summers at Sils-Maria and wished to be buried there (NEH, *Humanities*, 2012). The closing line's year is right.
