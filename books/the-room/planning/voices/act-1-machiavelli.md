## Voice card

AGENTS.md now overrides the global decisions G3, G6, G7, G10, and G11 wherever they differ: a sharp line beats a style rule, budgets are per part, pace rules apply, and narrators may say what a composite role sees, feels, or fails to notice.

### Narrative stance

- **Person.** First person singular from the first line to the last: "I, Niccolo." He never calls himself Machiavelli; only the italic attribution line and the epigraph citations name him. Every "Machiavelli wrote" becomes "I wrote", every "Machiavelli would recognize" becomes "I recognize", and "Machiavelli saw Savonarola preach" becomes "I heard the friar preach".
- **Tense.** Present for the meeting and for his rules ("Monday morning. The Author enters the room."; "I say that a new prince..."). Past for his own life and for his examples ("I was sent to the Duke"; "Hiero did away with the old militia"). The perfect for what he has seen across the centuries ("I have seen this before"). The future only for a prediction he is willing to be judged by.
- **Addressee.** Like his little book, the act is offered to a prince who will not read it: the committee, as Lorenzo was. Inside his precepts he turns to "you", the second person of his rules ("though you may have fortresses"), meaning anyone who would bring a new paper into the room. The Author is always "he", never "you".
- **Mode.** He tells the story as a witness and counselor. He reports what can be seen and heard in the room, what the public record shows, and what the Author would tell a confidant, the way the Duke told him his plans. He may say what the Chair or another composite role perceives, experiences, or fails to notice when it sharpens a scene ("The Chair does not experience this as cruelty. He experiences it as scheduling."). He never states what the Chair or the Architect privately knew, and never what the Architect knew about his tradeoff. He marks any guess at another man's motive as his own judgment ("as I believe"). One such mark is enough; he does not stack them (AGENTS.md, Hedging).
- **The act's question.** How many users will actually benefit? The users are not in the room when the decisions are made, and this act shows it by counting: the room counts hands, delegates, and polls, and never the users a design would serve. Niccolo puts the question as a count, at most three times in the act: the first question in V, the uncalled poll that closes VII, and the occasion in IX. He never poses another act's question: the cost, whether the problem is real, whether the existing solutions are good enough, whether it belongs in the standard at all, or how anyone knows the users want it.
- **His own works.** He calls The Prince "my little work" or "the little book I wrote for Lorenzo". He may use the title The Prince, the Italian title the printers gave it after his death, but he prefers his own names for it. He cites it by chapter in words: "the sixth chapter of my little work", "my chapter on fortresses". The letter is "a letter I wrote to my friend Francesco Vettori". Of republics he says only that he reasoned on them at length elsewhere, as chapter II does.
- **The other five.** He died before any of them wrote, so he names none of them and quotes none of them. If a cross-reference is ever needed, he uses the book's epithets: the philosopher, the clerk, the naturalist, and the analyst, who stays unnamed. The book fixes no epithet for the author of Act Six, so Act One does not refer to him. The others call Niccolo the Florentine.
- **His contemporaries.** Savonarola is named at his first mention, as chapter VI of The Prince itself names "Frate Girolamo Savonarola", and is "the friar" after that; the multitude the friar preached to may be "the masses", while "multitude" stays inside the chapter VI quotation and its callback. The others go by title, as he wrote them: "the Duke" or "Duke Valentino" for Cesare Borgia, "Pope Julius" for Julius II, "Lorenzo" for the dedicatee.

### Sentence architecture

- **Division first.** A section opens by sorting the case into kinds and then takes each kind in turn: states are republics or principalities; arms are one's own, mercenary, auxiliary, or mixed.
- **One ruling in the first person.** "I say, then, that...", "I judge...", "I conclude, therefore, that...". One per section is enough.
- **Period, then point.** A long sentence of balanced clauses joined by "for", "and", "so that", "unless", often in threes ("nothing more difficult to handle, nor more doubtful of success, nor more dangerous to manage"), then a short sentence or maxim that closes it.
- **Examples in pairs.** An ancient example beside one from his own times, or a great example followed by "a lesser one" (Hiero after Moses). In this act the second example is the room.
- **Proportions, not hedges.** "Half, or a little less"; "more or less difficulty, as he has more or less virtu". Where he is sure he says so: "this never fails, and it is a general rule."
- **Plain signposts.** "I will leave aside...", "I want to add a lesser example", "But let us come to...".
- **Antithesis inside one sentence.** "Let them not accuse fortune but their own sloth." The split "It is not X. It is Y." and "not because X but because Y" follow the AGENTS.md per-part budgets (up to five "It is not X. It is Y." splits, counted in the strict form, and one or two "not because" sentences), spent on the sharpest lines; any other antithesis stays inside one sentence.
- **English register.** Plain modern words with the cadence of a chancery letter: semicolons, "therefore", "for", "hence". No archaism ("doth", "thee") and no slang ("loser", "on board").
- **Budget spends.** "It is not X. It is Y." (cap five, strict form): II "It does not merely add to the existing design. It replaces it.", III "I judged them by effects.", V "He experiences it as scheduling.", V "It was political.", and VI "The Author does not play the fox. He does not play the lion. He plays only the man". Not counted: the V triad, which is protected; IX "The Chair did not lose his state. He is still the Chair.", whose second sentence confirms the first rather than correcting it; and IX "But I did not write it for Lorenzo alone. I wrote it for anyone who...", which widens the first sentence rather than correcting it (a stricter count makes six). "Not because" (cap two): VI "not because he is wrong" and IX "not because they are right". "Devastating" (cap one): V's first question. "Precise" and "precision": none.
- **Merges.** AGENTS.md allows two per part, and never on a sharp standalone line. Act One keeps V "The room has thinned: eight people have left for parallel sessions or coffee or phone calls." and VIII "The Author's virtu has not changed; his knowledge of the ground has." Every other short semicolon or colon merge in the act is split. Semicolons and colons in long periodic sentences, in lists, and before a quotation stay; they are the chancery cadence above.
- **Anaphora.** Runs of sentences that open on the same words are his signature, as in the chapter XX catalog that serves as VII's epigraph ("others have... some have..."). AGENTS.md holds such devices to about four per act unless the voice file records them, and this act may use more: it carries about two dozen runs of three or more sentences, among them the opening's "I saw it", III's "He facilitates. He organizes. He articulates. He reflects." and "is the power", V's "Different is", VI's "The promise says" and "The power says", VII's "He decides" and "He thinks", and IX's "He still", "He understands", and "He will". Keep a run where it counts or drums. Avoid stacking runs in adjacent paragraphs; III (the "Every", "He is", and "is the power" paragraphs) and IX ("He understands" beside "He will") already come close.

### Signature moves

1. **He divides before he judges.**
   - Original: "Dico adunque, che l’armi, con le quali un Principe difende il suo Stato, o le sono proprie, o le sono mercenarie, o ausiliarie, o miste." (*Il Principe*, chapter XII)
   - Translation: "I say, then, that the arms with which a prince defends his state are either his own, or mercenary, or auxiliary, or mixed."
2. **He judges in the first person and measures instead of hedging.**
   - Original: "giudico potere esser vero, che la fortuna sia arbitra della metà delle azioni nostre, ma che ancora ella ne lasci governare l’altra metà, o poco meno, a noi." (*Il Principe*, chapter XXV)
   - Translation: "I judge it may be true that fortune is the arbiter of half our actions, but that she still leaves the other half, or a little less, for us to govern."
3. **He follows the effectual truth, not the imagined republic.**
   - Original: "mi è parso più conveniente andare dietro alla verità effettuale della cosa, che all’immaginazione di essa" (*Il Principe*, chapter XV)
   - Translation: "it has seemed more fitting to me to go after the effectual truth of the thing than after the imagination of it"
4. **He sets an example from his own times beside the ancients.**
   - Original: "Moisè, Ciro, Teseo, e Romulo non arebbono potuto fare osservare lungamente le loro costituzioni, se fussero stati disarmati, come ne’ nostri tempi intervenne a Frate Girolamo Savonarola" (*Il Principe*, chapter VI)
   - Translation: "Moses, Cyrus, Theseus, and Romulus could not have made their constitutions observed for long had they been unarmed, as happened in our own times to Fra Girolamo Savonarola"
5. **He takes a short image from a craft or from nature, applies it in one clause, and moves on.**
   - Original: "e fare come gli arcieri prudenti, ai quali parendo il luogo, dove disegnano ferire, troppo lontano, e cognoscendo fino a quanto arriva la virtù del loro arco, pongono la mira assai più alta, che il luogo destinato" (*Il Principe*, chapter VI)
   - Translation: "and do as prudent archers do, who, when the place they mean to hit seems too far, and knowing how far the virtu of their bow reaches, set their aim much higher than the mark"
6. **He raises the disputed question himself and answers it at once.**
   - Original: "Nasce da questo una disputa: *s’egli è meglio essere amato che temuto, o temuto che amato*. Rispondesi, che si vorrebbe essere l’uno e l’altro" (*Il Principe*, chapter XVII)
   - Translation: "From this a dispute arises: whether it is better to be loved than feared, or feared than loved. The answer is that one would want to be both"
7. **He testifies to what he saw and heard.**
   - Original: "Ed egli mi disse ne’ dì che fu creato Iulio II, che avea pensato a tutto quello che potesse nascere morendo il Padre, e a tutto aveva trovato rimedio" (*Il Principe*, chapter VII)
   - Translation: "And he told me, in the days when Julius II was made pope, that he had thought of everything that could happen when his father died, and had found a remedy for everything"
8. **He writes from below.**
   - Original: "così come coloro che disegnano i paesi, si pongono bassi nel piano a considerare la natura de’ monti e de’ luoghi alti, e per considerare quella de’ bassi si pongono alti sopra i monti; similmente, a cognoscer bene la natura de’ popoli bisogna esser Principe, ed a cognoscer bene quella de’ Principi conviene essere popolare." (*Il Principe*, dedication)
   - Translation: "just as those who draw landscapes set themselves low on the plain to consider the nature of mountains and high places, and set themselves high on the mountains to consider the low, so to know well the nature of peoples one must be a prince, and to know well the nature of princes one must be of the people."

### Words

- **Use:** state; principality; the new prince and the hereditary prince; arms, one's own arms, the arms of others; fortune, as "she"; virtu, the book's unaccented spelling, glossed once as the strength that takes and holds a state, the way a bow has virtu; necessity; occasion; new orders; the great and the people in quotations, the peerage and the users in the room; humors; fortress; barriers and embankments; the effectual truth; the uncounted, Act One's word for the users; the masses, for the multitude the friar preached to; "I say", "I judge", "I conclude".
- **Avoid:** "visionary" (the word is prophet); "loser", "won through", "on board"; "pre-socialize" (say he wins friends before the meeting); management words such as stakeholders, incentives, optics, dynamics, leverage, institutional capital, and gravitational field; the other voices' words: the philosopher's herd and ressentiment, the clerk's castle and officials, the naturalist's crowd, pack, and sting, the analyst's unconscious, shadow, and projection, and Act Six's ceremony and legitimacy; British spellings (honoured, labelled, defence, dykes); "door" anywhere but the contrast between a fortress and a door; "the vulgar" for anyone in or outside the room. "Devastating" and "precise" or "precision" are not banned; they follow the AGENTS.md per-part budgets, at most one each.
- **He never:**
  - names himself in the third person;
  - praises or blames a man for his intentions; he judges by effects;
  - states what another man privately knew; he may say what a composite role perceives, experiences, or fails to notice when it sharpens a scene;
  - explains an image after applying it;
  - complains of his fortune beyond one sentence, as the dedication does;
  - inflates his rank; he was a secretary sent on missions, "a man of low and humble station" in his own dedication;
  - turns his general sayings about men ("ungrateful, fickle") on the users, the delegates, the implementers, or the host cities;
  - uses the close of chapter XXV, where fortune is a woman to be beaten and struck, or any image of that kind, which the book's boundaries bar.

### Drift tests

1. Does Niccolo speak as "I" throughout, with the name Machiavelli appearing only in epigraph attribution lines?
2. Does every general rule in the paragraph stand on a named example, from his own times, from the ancients, or from this meeting?
3. Does the paragraph judge the Chair, the Architect, and the room by their positions and effects, say what a composite perceives or experiences only where it sharpens the scene, and never say what the Chair or the Architect privately knew?
4. Does the paragraph advance by division, example, and conclusion, with no sentence restating the one before it?
5. Is the paragraph free of management and psychology words, of the other five voices' vocabulary, and of any image explained beyond one applying clause, does the part stay within the AGENTS.md per-part budgets, and does the paragraph end on its sharpest sentence?
6. If the paragraph asks the users' question, is it this act's count of how many users will benefit, and is it one of no more than three in the act?

### Narrative facts

- **His name for the book.** In the letter to Vettori he calls it "uno opuscolo *De principatibus*" and "questo mio opuscolo". The title *Il Principe* first appears in the printings of 1532, in Rome and in Florence, five years after his death. Sources: letter to Francesco Vettori, 10 December 1513, paragraphs 5 and 7; Treccani, *Enciclopedia machiavelliana*, "Principe, Il".
- **The dedication.** The dedication names Lorenzo di Piero de' Medici, who never employed its author. The act says plainly that Niccolo dedicated it to Lorenzo and that Lorenzo never read it, under the license AGENTS.md gives the narrators' own lives. Sources: *Il Principe*, dedication; Marriott, introduction, "Literature and Death".
- **He feared it would not be read.** In the same letter, weighing whether to present it, he fears that Giuliano will not even read it, and that a papal secretary, Ardinghelli, will take the credit for it. Source: letter to Vettori, paragraph 7.
- **His service.** He was secretary of the Second Chancery and of the Ten from 1498 until the Medici returned in 1512, and was dismissed by decree of 7 November 1512. His own count, in the letter, is "quindici anni" spent "a studio all'arte dello stato", fifteen years at the study of the art of the state. Sources: Marriott, introduction, "Office" and "Literature and Death"; letter to Vettori, paragraph 7.
- **Released, not acquitted.** Accused of a part in a plot against the Medici, he was imprisoned and tortured; the new Medici pope, Leo X, procured his release. "Found innocent" should become "released". Source: Marriott, introduction, "Literature and Death".
- **His good faith.** In the same letter, the author of chapter XVIII writes that, having always kept faith, he will not learn now to break it, and that his poverty is witness to his faith and goodness. Source: letter to Vettori, paragraph 7.
- **He heard the friar.** He heard Savonarola preach, and the act says so in one plain clause. Sources: Treccani, *Enciclopedia machiavelliana*, "Becchi, Ricciardo"; Portale Machiavelli, *Lettere*.
- **The principality he served.** In his last years he served the Medici. Cardinal Giulio de' Medici commissioned his history of Florence in 1520, and in May 1526 he became chancellor of the Procuratori delle mura, a board of five citizens charged for one year with fortifying Florence's walls. So the man who wrote that the best fortress is not to be hated by the people kept the Medici's walls at the end. Sources: Marriott, introduction, "Literature and Death"; Sistema Archivistico Nazionale, "Procuratori delle mura della citta di Firenze"; Treccani, *Enciclopedia machiavelliana*, "Scritti di governo".
- **His death.** When the Medici fell in 1527 he hurried home hoping to recover his old secretaryship under the restored republic, fell ill, and died in Florence that June. Source: Marriott, introduction, "Literature and Death".
