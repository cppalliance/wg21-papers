**RULE OF THUMB**

**The shape of a paper that passes.** Problem statement. Prior art and a survey of earlier solutions and related papers. Your design. Alternative designs and their honest trade-offs. Committee discussion so far, both sides fairly. Implementation and usage experience. Wording. Acknowledgements naming your reviewers and where they come from.

**FROM THE RULEBOOK**

"Be example-based ... Be principle-based ... Show design alternatives considered ... ## BOX 5: WATCH OUT

- status: kept inline
- section: 11.8 Scope, Dependencies, and the High Bar

**WATCH OUT**

An idea with no examples, alternatives, or proof wastes the room's time. The bar is cultural, not enforced, but thin papers still go nowhere. Bring worked examples and an implementation, even on your first paper.

## BOX 6: THE SHORT VERSION

- status: dropped
- section: 11.8 Scope, Dependencies, and the High Bar

**THE SHORT VERSION**

Heard is easy; yes is hard. Follow the shape that passes: problem, prior art and history, design, honest alternatives, the discussion so far, experience, wording, acknowledgements. Examples, principles, and alternatives are SD-4's own three requirements. Lead with a one-sentence finding, show an honest Tony Table, steel-man yourself twice, and bring an implementation plus a feature-test macro. Wording is law: shall versus should, labels not numbers, and a CWG or LWG expert over your shoulder the first time. One topic per paper. The default answer is no, and the language is guarded harder than the library.
