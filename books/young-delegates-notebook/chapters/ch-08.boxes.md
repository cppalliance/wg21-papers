## BOX 2: FROM THE RULEBOOK

- status: dropped
- section: 9.1 The Culture of Consensus

**FROM THE RULEBOOK**

"Consensus: General agreement, characterized by the absence of sustained opposition to substantial issues by any important part of the concerned interests and by a process that involves seeking to take into account the views of all parties concerned and to reconcile any conflicting arguments. NOTE Consensus need not imply unanimity." (ISO/IEC Guide 2, as quoted in [SD-4](https://isocpp.org/std/standing-documents/sd-4-wg21-practices-and-procedures)). SD-4 adds that consensus "is not a tyranny of the majority" and "is not a tyranny of the minority."

## BOX 3: FROM THE RULEBOOK

- status: dropped
- section: 9.3 Reading Poll Results

**FROM THE RULEBOOK**

"A proposal normally advances if there are more than twice as many in favor of a proposal as against, after discussion of the concerns of those voting against and possibly a re-poll to see if opinions have improved. This is true even if a large number vote Neutral, though it can be concerning if a majority of all those voting vote Neutral." ([SD-4](https://isocpp.org/std/standing-documents/sd-4-wg21-practices-and-procedures), Subgroup polls)

**FROM THE RULEBOOK**

"'I didn't have time to read the document' is not sufficient reason to oppose (provided the paper was submitted on time)." "People who were not sufficiently motivated to take part in the discussion should feel obliged to at least stay neutral." "Similarly, if your position has been discussed and voted down in WG, then you should at least vote neutral in plenary, to at least abide by the group's position." ([P2000R5](https://www.open-std.org/jtc1/sc22/wg21/docs/papers/2026/p2000r5.pdf), "Directions for ISO C++", § 5.1, Trust)

## BOX 5: WATCH OUT

- status: kept inline
- section: 9.5 When to Vote and When to Abstain

**WATCH OUT**

Voting with the room on a paper you didn't read is a quiet mistake. Your passive vote still counts under consensus, so it muddies the signal, and four kinds of non-opinion are already hiding in the neutral and in-favour columns. Don't add to the problem. Read it or don't vote.

## BOX 7: RULE OF THUMB

- status: dropped
- section: 9.8 The Plenary: Where Features Enter the Draft

**RULE OF THUMB**

If you can't live with a decision, go to your head of delegation first, then post on the reflector by 5pm the evening before plenary. The deadline is for getting the motion changed or pulled; your country's sustained objection is carried by your delegation, not by the thread. Saturday morning is too late by design.

## BOX 8: THE SHORT VERSION

- status: dropped
- section: 9.8 The Plenary: Where Features Enter the Draft

**THE SHORT VERSION**

Consensus is the absence of sustained opposition, not a majority. Subgroups may take polls and everyone present can vote.2:1 support usually means the poll passes.. Plenary decides by unanimous consent or a three-way poll the convener judges; the only formal votes are the national ballots. Read it or don't vote: neutral is for the read-it-but-undecided. Direction and design approval are not promises to ship. A minority’s objections can prevent consensus, but a majority may still proceed once those objections have been heard and considered.  Objections must be escalated by 5pm the day before plenary to count; your delegation, not the reflector, is what sustains it.
