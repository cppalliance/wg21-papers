- status: dropped
- section: 13.1 Before the Room: Presocialization and the Collaboration Ladder

**RULE OF THUMB**

**The collaboration ladder.** Review their paper, carefully and in writing. Review the next revision. Help with a section or an implementation. Co-author. Then bring your own paper, with their names in the acknowledgements and their voices in the room. Climb it in that order; it does not work from the top.

## BOX 3: FROM THE RULEBOOK

- status: dropped

**FROM THE RULEBOOK**

"Try to read several papers from each mailing that is not aimed at 'your WG'." "Try to spend at least one day each meeting in a WG that isn't 'your own'." ([P2000R5](https://www.open-std.org/jtc1/sc22/wg21/docs/papers/2026/p2000r5.pdf), "Directions for ISO C++", § 5, Process issues) "While co-authorship is a strong signal of consensus, it is not required. A single author citing feedback from diverse reviewers (e.g., 'Thanks to X from Embedded and Y from HFT for review') is equally valid." ([P4024R0](https://www.open-std.org/jtc1/sc22/wg21/docs/papers/2026/p4024r0.pdf), Rationale and Breadth)

## BOX 4: WATCH OUT

- status: kept inline
- section: 13.4 When the Poll Goes Against You

**WATCH OUT**

Getting direction from a room and then ignoring it stalls your paper. The design rooms exist to set direction, and working against it keeps the opposition alive. Follow the guidance, or reopen it in that same room with a paper, but don't route around it.

## BOX 5: RULE OF THUMB

- status: dropped
- section: 13.7 How Reputation Works, and How the Presenter Is Judged

**RULE OF THUMB**

Be the person who concedes the fair point quickly and holds the important one calmly. That reputation is worth more than any single paper.

## BOX 6: THE SHORT VERSION

- status: dropped
- section: 13.10 The Emotional Side

**THE SHORT VERSION**

Win it in the hallway before the room, and start years earlier by reviewing other people's papers, then co-authoring, then proposing. Find or be a champion. Frame the idea to the Direction paper. A poll against you is feedback, not death, but don't re-litigate without new evidence. Carry a back-pocket design and know your max-min. Reputation compounds; so does procedural momentum. Think in years, mind the bandwidth and the bubble, and pace yourself.
