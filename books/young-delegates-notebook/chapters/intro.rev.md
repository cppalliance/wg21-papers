**A Young Delegate's Notebook**

*A Newcomer's Path into WG21 and the Standardization of C++*

This book is a practical guide for anyone who wants to help shape C++ but has never set foot in a committee meeting. The chapters build on one another, but you don’t need to finish the book before you can contribute. Every paper number is a live link, and every named resource points somewhere real.

*Búzios edition, November 2026. Officer names, fees, attendance figures, and paper revisions are stated as of September 2026. The “As of 2026” boxes flag information to check again before relying on it. The current roster is at [isocpp.org/std/the-committee](https://isocpp.org/std/the-committee). The standing documents and the Direction Group’s papers are revised over time. Committee practice can change before either is updated. Watch for updates, and when this notebook and the current SD-4 disagree, SD-4 wins.*

## Introduction

*"I have not found among my possessions anything I hold more dear or esteem so highly as my knowledge of the actions of great men, learned through long experience of modern events and continual study of ancient ones, which, having thought about and examined with great diligence, I have now set down in this little book and send to Your Magnificence."*

*Niccolò Machiavelli, dedicatory letter,* [The Prince](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/1232) *(adapted from the W. K. Marriott translation)*

I wrote this for you, not for the experts who've sat in these rooms for twenty years but for you, the one who just found out that a group of volunteers decides what goes into C++, and wondered how to join them.

Machiavelli gave a prince the one thing he valued most: what he'd learned watching powerful people up close. This notebook works the same way, smaller and plainer. It's the worn, marked-up notebook I wish someone had pressed into my hands before my first meeting.

The group is real, and you can join it. It's called WG21, and it's [the committee](https://isocpp.org/std/the-committee) that owns the C++ standard. Around two hundred people show up to each meeting, argue, vote, and ship a new version of the language every three years. These are some of the most careful people you'll ever meet. They'll spend an hour on the placement of a single word. They do it because the stakes are high, and you'll come to understand why.

### Who This Notebook Is For, and What It Is Not

This is for the newcomer. Maybe you've never been to a meeting, or you've been to a few and still feel lost in the room. Either way, you're in the right place. You don’t need to know every rule in C++ or to have written a compiler. You do need curiosity and the patience to keep showing up.

This is an opinionated notebook, not a textbook or a reference manual. It values stability over novelty and puts the people who use C++ ahead of the people who design it.

When the evidence is thin, it says so. Where the committee has written a rule down, the rule is quoted, so you can check me.

### How This Notebook Is Laid Out

- **In This Chapter** opens every chapter with what you'll be able to do by the end of it.
- **From the Rulebook** quotes or summarizes rules and procedures, with links to the sources.
- **Rule of Thumb** is the one-line version you'll remember in the room.
- **Try This Today** is something you can do before you've finished the chapter.
- **Watch Out** marks the trap newcomers fall into at exactly that point.
- **As of 2026** flags information that may change, such as names, fees, and dates.
- **From One Delegate's Notebook** tells a true story from the committee's record, without names, where a rule cost someone something.
- **The Short Version** closes each chapter in a few lines.

### What Each Chapter Covers

The book moves in order, from "What is this thing?" to "How do I take part well?"

- **Chapter 1** shows you why the standard matters and why every change you make is close to permanent.
- **Chapter 2** introduces the committee and the doors you can walk in through.
- **Chapter 3** is about the door closest to home: your own country's national body.
- **Chapter 4** explains how papers work, because the paper is the unit of all the work.
- **Chapter 5** shows you how to take part from your desk, long before you ever travel.
- **Chapter 6** maps the rooms and groups, so you know who decides what.
- **Chapter 7** helps you plan, fund, and survive your first meeting.
- **Chapter 8** covers how to behave, speak, and find your footing once you're in the room.
- **Chapter 9** explains how decisions get made and what a vote really means.
- **Chapter 10** follows the draft out of the committee and into the countries' ballot.
- **Chapter 11** lays out what a proposal must contain to earn the committee's time.
- **Chapter 12** walks through the tools, the format, and the deadlines for your own paper.
- **Chapter 13** covers the long work of building support and surviving feedback.
- **Chapter 14** names the deep design constraints that decide what can and can't ship.
- **Chapter 15** collects the traps on one page, so you can step around them.

One habit matters more than any fact in this notebook: Stay skeptical. The committee runs on persuasion, and persuasion can carry you past your own judgment. Hold onto it. These four lines capture the spirit of this notebook:

> Keep your wits about you. Be skeptical. Demand evidence. Put the users first.

Welcome to *A Young Delegate’s Notebook*. I'm glad you came.
