## BOX 1: FROM THE RULEBOOK

- status: dropped
- section: 14. C++ Design Principles

**FROM THE RULEBOOK**

The Direction Group's standing paper says of a new feature that D&E recommends "not to accept a feature that doesn't solve two apparently unrelated problems", and lists the questions to ask first: what problems the feature solves, what alternatives are possible, what related features should be considered at the same time, how well it fits the style of the language and library, and whether it is aimed at a few experts or at application code. ([P2000R5](https://www.open-std.org/jtc1/sc22/wg21/docs/papers/2026/p2000r5.pdf), "Directions for ISO C++", § 5.4, "Details" vs. Design)

## BOX 3: WATCH OUT

- status: dropped
- section: 14.4 ABI: The Invisible Constraint

**WATCH OUT**

If your proposal touches the layout of an existing type, or changes what a function is mangled to, expect the ABI question before any other. Answer it in the paper, not from the floor.

## BOX 4: THE SHORT VERSION

- status: dropped
- section: 14.5 Freestanding vs. Hosted

**THE SHORT VERSION**

The rules are Stroustrup's, in The Design and Evolution of C++; read it first. Bless what already works. Old code must keep working, and one type has to serve everyone, which is why jthread had to be a second type. Don't tax people who don't use your feature. ABI kills more proposals than anything; since Prague 2020 it is guarded but not promised. Remember the freestanding world.
