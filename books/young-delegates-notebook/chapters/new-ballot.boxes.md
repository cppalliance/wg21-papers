## BOX 2: TABLE (4 columns, 7 rows)

- status: dropped
- section: 10.1 Two Ballots, Not One

| **Stage** | **Who votes** | **Length** | **What it decides** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Working draft | WG21 plenary, individual experts | Every meeting | What goes into the text |
| CD (Committee Draft) | SC22 national bodies | Committee's choice of 8, 12 or 16 weeks | Comments that shape the final text |
| DIS (Draft International Standard) | ISOnational bodies | 12 weeks (8 for a revised DIS) | Approval: at least two-thirds of P-members voting in favour, no more than a quarter of votes cast against |
| FDIS (Final Draft) | ISO national bodies | 8 weeks | Only if the DIS drew negative votes; otherwise skipped |
| Publication | ISO |  | The International Standard |
| **FROM THE RULEBOOK** <br> Under ISO's rules the DIS ballot runs twelve weeks. It passes if at least two-thirds of the participating members who vote are in favour and no more than a quarter of all votes cast are against; abstentions don't count. A final FDIS ballot of eight weeks follows only if the DIS drew negative votes. When the DIS passes clean, the text goes straight to publication, which is what recent C++ standards have done. (ISO/IEC Directives Part 1, 2.6 and 2.7) |  |  |  |

## BOX 7: THE SHORT VERSION

- status: dropped
- section: 10.7 Your Country's Vote

**THE SHORT VERSION**

The CD is where countries change the text; the DIS is yes or no. A DIS needs two-thirds of P-members voting in favour and no more than a quarter against; the FDIS is skipped when the DIS is clean and no technical changes are required . A comment is a small paper with a proposed fix. No means "not at all," and there is no way back after the ballot. Don't ask for new features or re-fight settled points. Vet comments as one national voice.
