## BOX 5: WATCH OUT

- status: dropped
- section: 3.3 How to Join Yours

**WATCH OUT**

The day your name enters the Global Directory you hold something people want: a vote, in a room where consensus is counted by hand. Authors with a paper on the agenda will notice a new member of a national delegation before the new member notices them, and some will make a point of being helpful, friendly, and persuasive in the week before a poll. None of that is improper; it is how the committee talks to itself. But be aware of it. Let people earn your vote with evidence and a paper you have read, not with a free lunch, and never promise a vote before you have done the reading.

**WATCH OUT**

SCC’s grounds for removing members include missed meetings, three consecutive unanswered ballots, and twelve months without accessing its online platforms. Members who are removed also lose access to online platforms for standards work with SCC, ISO, and IEC. Check your national body’s participation requirements, respond to its notices, and stay involved. Similarly, the BSI asks its members to complete an attendance sheet at monthly meetings so it knows who is still alive and engaged.

## BOX 9: AS OF 2026

- status: dropped
- section: 3.6 A Worked Example: The Canadian Door

**AS OF 2026**

**The Canadian door in one box.** Mirror committee: MC/ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 22 (Standards Council of Canada), one committee for all of SC22, with a chair, a vice-chair, and a head of delegation per language. Meets four times a year for an hour. Fee: none, but activity is required to stay on the roster, and SCC now tracks it by your logins to its website; drop off the roster and you drop out of the Global Directory. Who qualifies: Canadian citizens, residents, and employees of Canadian-domiciled companies. How: the SCC "Join a mirror committee" page, with a CV and a motivation letter; write to the C++ head of delegation first. Position-forming: a Zoom caucus in the hour before the Saturday plenary, open to remote and in-room members, and a live vote sheet, with each member's employer known to the group. The chair of the mirror committee serves fixed terms with a nine-year limit under SCC's rules, so the names change; the structure doesn't.

## BOX 12: THE SHORT VERSION

- status: dropped
- section: 3.7 Which Door Is Yours

**THE SHORT VERSION**

Countries are the members and you are their expert. Each country's mirror committee decides whom it accredits and what the country thinks, by its own rules: In a company-based body the companies vote; in an expert-based body you do. Join through the NB where you live, say which working groups you want to join (you may be joining the whole SC22 mirror, not a C++ panel), allow weeks, and get into the Global Directory. A new vote is courted; make people earn it. Being on any national body, paid or free, carries two duties: Be informed and be active. Your head of delegation is the one the convener may ask at the Saturday plenary, so tell them before the plenary; Canada does it in a Zoom caucus in the hour just before, where remote and in-room members alike report from the rooms they attended. Nothing is a national position until your delegation has talked. Canada's version: one committee for all of SC22, quarterly meetings, no fee, a mentor per newcomer, a caucus before plenary, and a live vote sheet.
