## Abstract

Contracts entered C++26 through procedural moves that broke no rule and left no record that the sustained technical opposition to the design was ever reconciled.

WG21 decides by consensus, which ISO defines as the reconciliation of conflicting arguments and not a count of votes. The Contracts proposal advanced on a roadmap that made forward motion the default, with the Technical Specification path closed, papers merged faster than many members could review them, and a long series of incremental polls whose accumulation was treated as a mandate no single vote had granted. When the design was carried over a sustained Strongly-Against minority, the poll and its recorded result, "Consensus," were the whole of what entered the record, never a finding that the opposition had been answered, and the papers that report the history do not always match the meeting record.

None of it broke a rule, which is why the working group cannot catch it from inside: a process built to produce consensus on time will produce it, whether or not the underlying design was audited. The one audit that ISO places outside the working group is the National Body ballot, and the C++ Alliance's position is that the National Bodies vote No on the C++26 DIS ballot, with technical comments, and return the draft for the evaluation it skipped, at the cost of one cycle. The same maneuvers are already running again on a successor proposal, which is why the ballot check should be exercised now rather than deferred to a later cycle.

The authors provide information and serve at the pleasure of the committee. This paper requests no floor time, no poll, and no committee action; it states a position and two recommendations, addressed to the National Bodies and the convener.

## 1. Introduction: The Alliance Position

The C++ Alliance's position is that the National Bodies should vote No on the C++26 DIS ballot, with technical comments, as [ISO/IEC Directives Part 1, clause 2.6.2](https://www.iso.org/sites/directives/current/consolidated/index.html) provides, and return the draft for the technical evaluation the Contracts design did not receive. The Alliance further recommends that the convener state that the committee's officers will not penalize any delegate or National Body for casting that vote.

The case for a No vote rests on two independent grounds. A National Body persuaded only that the Contracts design is not ready, on the technical record alone and setting aside any account of how it advanced, has sufficient basis to vote No. The procedural findings that follow explain how a design carrying sustained technical opposition reached the ballot without that opposition being reconciled; they strengthen the case but are not a precondition for it.

This paper first states the model of consensus determination it uses (Section 2), and then contributes:

1. an account of the procedural path by which Contracts entered C++26 (Section 3);
2. an analysis of how vote counts came to stand in for the chair's reasoned determination (Section 4); and
3. the constitutional basis for the National Body ballot as the remaining external check (Section 5).

The paper assumes that the procedural moves it describes were compliant with SD-4 and the ISO Directives; it does not allege that any rule was broken. It further assumes that the poll tallies it cites are accurate as recorded.

## 2. How Consensus Is Supposed to Work

WG21 decides by consensus, not by vote. The chair, a subject-matter expert, weighs the arguments of every side. Where they can be reconciled, the chair reconciles them. Where they cannot be reconciled, the chair decides, and may decide against the numerical majority. This is deliberate: WG21's own guidance states that "the chair's determination of consensus is authoritative, not the straw poll" and that "we make decisions by consensus, not majority" ([P2195R2](https://wg21.link/p2195r2)). ISO defines consensus as a process that takes all views into account and reconciles conflicting arguments, not a count of hands ([ISO/IEC Directives Part 1, clause 2.5.6](https://www.iso.org/sites/directives/current/consolidated/index.html)). The chair's authority is broad by design, and rightly so; a committee that decided technical questions by votes rather than expert judgment would be worse.

The counterpart of that authority is a recorded reason. Authority to declare consensus over a sustained objection is answerable only if the chair states why, and that record is what a body outside the working group can later weigh. ISO builds this review into the process: at the committee-draft stage it directs the leadership to register sustained opposition and resolve it in good faith, and at the ballot the National Bodies read the result and judge whether the call was sound. Authority to decide, a recorded rationale, and external review of that rationale are one mechanism; remove any part and the rest cannot function.

Read through that model, the record shows a process that kept the authority and dropped the accountability: consensus declared from vote ratios over sustained opposition, with no rationale recorded for anyone to review. The committee has substituted polls for the chair's reasoned determination. No one explicitly switched to polling; reconciliation has simply faded from practice. The remedy is to restore the review that always accompanied the chair's power (Section 5).

## 3. The Procedural Path of P2900

Every move in this section was available within SD-4 and the ISO Directives; none required a rule to be broken. The account below draws on the Contracts authors' own published rationale, [P2899](https://wg21.link/p2899), and on the recorded poll results.

**The Consensus Ratchet.** P2900 advanced through a long series of incremental polls, no one of them decisive, each merged change becoming the settled baseline the next poll built upon. [P2899](https://wg21.link/p2899) records dozens of such determinations, and the 63 papers in ten months counted by [P3443R0](https://wg21.link/p3443r0) fed that accumulation. No single poll ever asked the room to approve the design as a whole; the mandate was assembled one reversible-looking increment at a time, until the accumulation itself was treated as the mandate. The polls that did forward the design were themselves counts recorded as consensus over a standing minority: EWG sent P2900R11 to CWG for C++26 (Wroclaw, 2024-11-18: 25/17/0/3/12) and LEWG forwarded it the same week (Wroclaw, 2024-11-21: 23/9/1/0/5), each carrying a double-digit or five-vote Strongly-Against bloc and neither recording a finding that the opposition had been answered.

**The Reversed Burden.** Once the increments had accumulated, removing the feature required a fresh consensus the objectors could not assemble: the motion to remove P2900 from C++26 failed (Hagenberg, 2025-02: 9/8/3/19/41), so a divided room preserved the feature by default. Adding a feature to the standard and removing one from it both require consensus, and every accumulated approval shifted the burden further onto whoever wished to stop it, independent of the technical merits.

**The Roadmap Ratchet.** In November 2022 SG21 adopted a roadmap for a Contracts MVP targeting C++26, recorded in [P2899](https://wg21.link/p2899) with the clause that the roadmap will "need consensus in SG21 to revise" (Kona, 2022-11-12: 10/10/5/1/1). Once that clause was in force, forward motion was the default, and any proposal to slow down was itself a revision of the roadmap, subject to the same consensus determination the chair controlled. Starting the schedule cost one poll; stopping it required assembling a second consensus against the people who had set the first. The direction of travel was fixed before the design was settled.

**The Closed TS Path.** A Technical Specification would have let implementations ship Contracts and gather field experience before the design entered the International Standard, and SG21 twice declined that path (SG21, 2022-12: 15 for, 3 against, 0 abstaining, to follow the roadmap rather than pursue a TS; SG21, 2024-05-30: 1/3/3/12/10 against a TS). At the 2024 session the single pro-TS paper was outnumbered two to one by papers from the Contracts authors scheduled against it. With the TS route closed, the only remaining path was the IS pipeline, where the C++26 deadline supplied the time pressure that later complaints described. Gabriel Dos Reis, who favored more field experience, wrote that "rushing the current P2900 into the IS is among the worse mistakes we could make, ranking way ahead of a TS that permits evaluation of the various conjectures in P2900" ([SG21 reflector, 2024-05-23](https://lists.isocpp.org/sg21/2024/05/8894.php)). Co-author John Spicer, who chaired those sessions, has concluded that "P2900 doesn't make anything better, and makes quite a lot worse" ([ext reflector, 2025-10-24](https://lists.isocpp.org/ext/2025/10/26208.php)).

**Rebuttal by Volume.** Objections to P2900 were met with counter-papers longer than the objections and authored by the same people who controlled scheduling: [P3327R0](https://wg21.link/p3327r0) answered function-pointer concerns, and the eighteen-page [P3591R0](https://wg21.link/p3591r0) (by Berne and Doumler) and the twenty-two-author [P3846R0](https://wg21.link/p3846r0) answered design and national-body concerns. Presented alongside the objection and then put to a vote, these papers settled questions by volume and authorship rather than by substance. Co-author Ville Voutilainen has described the pattern as "multiple other such rebuttal papers that end up being dismissal papers" ([ext reflector, 2025-02-03](https://lists.isocpp.org/ext/2025/02/24718.php)).

**Compressed Review Window.** [P3443R0](https://wg21.link/p3443r0) recorded the tempo of the process: 63 papers handled in ten months, a majority of them merged into P2900 less than a week after publication. At that rate the only participants who could track each change were those working on Contracts full time, which left little review time for anyone else.

**Contradictory Consensus.** The treatment of contracts on virtual functions was decided and reversed repeatedly: EWG merged the caller/callee model ([P3097](https://wg21.link/p3097)) into [P2900](https://wg21.link/p2900) (St. Louis EWG, 2024-06-28: 18/15/5/1/2) and then removed virtual functions again (Hagenberg, 2025-02: 20/24/13/14/2). Each poll was recorded as consensus, yet the two pointed in opposite directions, so the result meant only what the room decided on a given day, not a stable technical position. This is not about the merits of virtual functions, which C++26 does not include; consensus was the recorded result of both a decision and its reversal within months. Bjarne Stroustrup, reviewing the surviving model, wrote that it "is novel, unique, untried, untested, and unimplemented. That alone should disqualify it from inclusion in an international standard" ([ext reflector, 2026-06-12](https://lists.isocpp.org/ext/2026/06/27121.php)).

## 4. When Votes Replace Judgment

Section 2 described the mechanism: the chair's reasoned determination, recorded, and open to review at the ballot. The determinations were made, but the reasoning behind them was not written down; consensus was read from the vote, and the vote is all that survives. That gap is not a broken rule.

**The reasons were never recorded.** At the subgroup level, where these determinations were made, [SD-4](https://isocpp.org/std/standing-documents/sd-4-wg21-practices-and-procedures) leaves the determination of consensus to the chair and requires no record of the reasons for it. A subgroup chair who declared consensus over sustained opposition therefore broke no rule. But the reconciliation the model calls for is nowhere shown in the record: consensus was declared over sustained Strongly-Against blocs with only the tally recorded and no finding that the opposition was addressed. Recording that finding is best practice, not a subgroup requirement, which is why the gap cannot be corrected from inside the process. Vinnie Falco, a co-author of this paper, put it precisely: "The chair may have excellent reasons for determining consensus after a 3.7:1 poll, but those reasons aren't documented. The number stands alone as the artifact" ([ext reflector, 2026-06-12](https://lists.isocpp.org/ext/2026/06/27123.php)).

**Consensus is not a majority vote.** As Section 2 set out, consensus turns on whether objections are resolved, not on the count; the IETF states the same for its own process ([RFC 7282](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc7282.html)). In the Contracts and P3100 records, consensus was instead called from vote ratios over sustained Strongly-Against minorities. Co-author J. Daniel Garc&iacute;a states the distinction plainly: "There is a difference between consensus and majority that should not be ignored" ([SG21 reflector, 2025-10-17](https://lists.isocpp.org/sg21/2025/10/11355.php)). Bjarne Stroustrup, reviewing the Brno straw poll, added that "5 strongly against is significant, and the opposition has been sustained" ([ext reflector, 2026-06-12](https://lists.isocpp.org/ext/2026/06/27121.php)).

**Poll Curation.** The record the Contracts authors publish does not always match the record the meeting kept. In the Contracts record, the committee's public poll record marked a Tokyo poll "not meant to be binding, but rather to offer a first set of feedback to the authors," and recorded no result for it (Tokyo EWG, 2024-03-20, Poll 8: 17/12/12/12/5, [cplusplus/papers #1648](https://github.com/cplusplus/papers/issues/1648#issuecomment-2009017161)). The Contracts authors' rationale paper reproduces that poll without the qualification, beside polls that carry a recorded result such as "Result: Consensus against" ([P2899R1](https://wg21.link/p2899r1); the same tally is also printed in [P3197R0](https://wg21.link/p3197r0)). From the published papers alone a reader cannot tell which polls were binding determinations and which the committee had explicitly set aside as non-binding feedback.

**The consequence of inaction: P3100.** The clearest sign that the working group cannot correct this from inside is that the same procedural maneuvers are running again, unchallenged, right now: a roadmap that made forward motion the default, incremental polls accumulated into a mandate, calendar pressure, and rebuttal by volume. P3100 is not part of C++26, and this paper asks the National Bodies to take no action on it; it is cited only as proof that the pattern of Section 3 is a repeatable method.

Timur Doumler and Joshua Berne, authors of the Contracts rationale [P2899R1](https://wg21.link/p2899r1) and [P2900R14](https://wg21.link/p2900r14), are the authors of [P3100R6](https://wg21.link/p3100r6). The method has been sharpened: a standing series of EWG telecons was voted into existence to review the paper one clause at a time (Sofia, 2025-06: 15/27/2/0/0), which commits the room's calendar in advance so competing designs cannot be scheduled. When the one poll that asked the room to move the paper forward failed (Croydon EWG, 2026-03-26: 1/17/10/18/14), P3100's own published history recorded only the same-day poll to continue case-by-case review (39/19/5/2/1) and omitted the adverse result. Once a technique carries a design over sustained opposition and meets no pushback, it becomes a precedent, and a precedent that succeeds is used again. Nothing here broke a rule, which is the point. The only thing that retires such a maneuver is resistance from outside the process that rewards it.

## 5. The Remedy: The National Body Ballot

A determination that is never required to be recorded or published can be tested only by an audience outside the working group, and that audience is the National Bodies at the ballot. A chair who can declare consensus over sustained opposition has every reason to reconcile in good faith when the determination will be read and weighed at the ballot; remove that oversight and the incentive disappears. The position and recommendations below restore the check.

**The Subtractive Last Line of Defense.** The National Body ballot is the only mechanism in the ISO system that can remove a feature after a working group has adopted it, and it sits outside the working group by design so that the people closest to the work are not the only ones who check it. A negative vote accompanied by technical comments is unconditionally valid. Approval requires both a two-thirds majority and no more than one quarter negative, so seven negatives among twenty-six voting P-members return the document ([ISO/IEC Directives Part 1, clauses 2.6.2, 2.6.3, 2.6.5](https://www.iso.org/sites/directives/current/consolidated/index.html)).

The power is real and recent: the trivial-relocation feature ([P2786](https://wg21.link/p2786)) was removed from C++26 after National Bodies objected, and [P4043R0](https://wg21.link/p4043r0) records more than twenty National Body comments on Contracts, several seeking its removal or deferral at the Committee Draft stage. Deferral under National Body pressure is ordinary practice in C++ itself: in 2008 the UK declared that "the BSI intends to vote 'No' on any C++0x FCD ballot that contains concepts" ([N2697](https://www.open-std.org/JTC1/SC22/WG21/docs/papers/2008/n2697.html)), and concepts were removed from C++0x the following year; and in 2006 UK-led National Body opposition defeated the C++/CLI fast-track ballot outright (11-9). A National Body that votes No with technical comments performs the oversight function the ballot exists for. J. Daniel Garc&iacute;a has warned that "there is a serious risk that for C++26 we get NB voting abstention or even against the IS," which "would be the first time for ISO 14882" ([ext reflector, 2026-03-08](https://lists.isocpp.org/ext/2026/03/26973.php)).

The return threshold is not the only reason to cast the vote. Every C++ DIS ballot from C++11 through C++23 passed unanimously; the C++26 CD ballot already broke that pattern, with three National Bodies voting No ([N5028](https://wg21.link/n5028)). A negative vote with technical comments, even short of the one-quarter that returns the draft, is a determination the committee cannot set aside: [clause 2.6.5](https://www.iso.org/sites/directives/current/consolidated/index.html) directs that "every attempt shall be made to resolve negative votes," which places the objection and the committee's answer on the permanent record and lowers the cost of the same scrutiny in later cycles.

The technical comments such a vote carries stand on their own. Stroustrup describes the novelty and lack of implementation, and Finland's Committee Draft comment and co-author Ville Voutilainen both pressed the absence of deployment experience ("Bring me deployment experience. Then we have a basis for voting", [ext reflector, 2026-07-17](https://lists.isocpp.org/ext/2026/07/27355.php)). Both are ballot substance independent of any procedural account. And returning the draft targets one contested feature at the cost of a single cycle; it does not reject reflection, execution, or the rest of C++26, which continue on their published paths.

The comment stage is not a substitute for the vote. The formal disposition of comments and its per-comment tracker are not public. The Contracts comments that were engaged at all were dispositioned by poll rather than by a recorded reconciliation: the response paper to Romania's comment RO 2-056 ([P4044R0](https://wg21.link/p4044r0)) failed (Croydon EWG, 2026-03-26, poll "Forward P4044R0 to CWG for inclusion in C++26 as a response to RO 2-056": 3/4/0/22/23, not consensus). A comment stage whose dispositions are neither published nor reconciled, only polled, cannot itself be the audit; were National Body comments answered by default with no change, the ballot would be a rubber stamp rather than a review. Because the comment stage resolves this way, the ballot vote is the check that remains.

**Removing the Penalty for Voting No.** A right that carries a social cost will be least used at the moment it is most needed. [SD-4](https://isocpp.org/std/standing-documents/sd-4-wg21-practices-and-procedures) states that a member who regularly uses the escalation process "erodes their credibility," a penalty the Directives nowhere attach to an unconditional ballot right ([ISO/IEC Directives Part 1, clauses 2.6.2, 5.1.2](https://www.iso.org/sites/directives/current/consolidated/index.html)), and the informal treatment of a No vote as disloyalty deters National Bodies from casting one. The convener has the standing to remove that deterrent. The C++ Alliance recommends that the convener state that the committee's officers will not penalize a delegate or a National Body for voting No, so that the last line of defense is available in fact and not only on paper. This is offered as a course the committee may adopt, not a demand, and it requests no floor time.

## 6. Conclusion

The set of procedural moves that carried Contracts into C++26 is now carrying P3100, and every one of them was permitted by the rules. That is the difficulty. A process followed exactly can still deliver a feature the record cannot show was reconciled with its sustained opposition, and the working group cannot detect this from the inside, because nothing inside the working group requires the reconciliation to be written down. The National Body ballot is the one review ISO placed outside the working group for exactly this case.

The C++ Alliance's position is that the National Bodies return C++26 for the technical evaluation it has not yet received, at the cost of a single cycle; this returns one contested feature, not reflection, execution, or the rest of the standard, and the work continues on published paths in the meantime. The Alliance further recommends that the convener state plainly that no officer will penalize a delegate or a National Body for a No vote, so that the check the system depends on is available in practice. If the draft is returned, C++ gains a feature that has been evaluated before it is locked into the standard and the ABI it ships. If the draft is rubber-stamped, C++ keeps paying for a defect that consensus procedure alone could not surface.
